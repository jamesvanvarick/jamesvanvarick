AAON, Inc.

NASDAQ: AAON · HVAC / Building Products

The call. I initiated on AAON in August 2025 at $88.29 as a moderate buy. AAON is a Tulsa-based maker of high-performance commercial HVAC, and the thesis centered on its BASX segment riding explosive AI data-center cooling demand — a ~40% three-year sales CAGR and backlog up 58% year-over-year — with the legacy brand supported by decarbonization tailwinds. Base-case DCF pointed to a $107 target.

What's played out. By the June 2026 update at $127.19, the stock had returned roughly 44% since the initiation. Q1 2026 was the inflection I was looking for: BASX backlog hit $1.62bn (+160% YoY), the AAON brand rebounded +41.6% after a rough 2025, and the three margin headwinds I'd flagged — the A2L refrigerant retooling, the Longview ERP go-live, and Memphis overhead — were working through as transitory. Updated fair value: $166.

Global Ship Lease, Inc.

NYSE: GSL · Containership Leasing

The call. I initiated on GSL in March 2025 at $23.18. It's a containership lessor with a mid-size and post-Panamax fleet levered to the faster-growing non-Mainlane trades. The thesis rested on deep contract cover ($1.9bn of contracted revenue), aggressive deleveraging, and a breakeven far below prevailing charter rates — all while the stock traded near 2.4x earnings. My dividend-discount base case was $68.

What's played out. By the July 2026 update at $41.31, the stock was up roughly 78%. The balance sheet became the strongest in the peer group — net debt around $222m, about 0.4x EBITDA, the lowest of five peers — with a $2.1bn backlog covering 100% of 2026 revenue days and 86% of 2027. The update reframes the thesis around downside protection: coverage holds even under a modeled 50% spot-rate shock, though the fleet is now the oldest in the group at 18.2 years.
